# The Intentional Computing Manifesto

> **Modern technology should expand human capability without consuming human attention.**

We believe technology is one of humanity's greatest tools.

Computers have given ordinary people capabilities that were once available only to governments, corporations, laboratories, and large institutions.

The internet gave us access to an extraordinary amount of knowledge.

AI is now giving individuals the ability to understand, create, program, research, translate, design, and build at a scale that would have been unimaginable only a few years ago.

We do not want to go backward.

We want to take the best of what technology has become and remove the parts that have made it worse to live with.

---

## 1. Technology should serve the person

A computer is a tool.

It should help someone accomplish something they have chosen to do.

It should not constantly attempt to determine what that person should watch, read, buy, think about, or do next.

We reject the idea that the success of software should primarily be measured by how long it keeps someone using it.

**A successful piece of software may be one that lets the user accomplish their goal and leave.**

---

## 2. Attention is a human resource

Time is finite.

Attention is finite.

A person's ability to concentrate is finite.

Software should therefore treat attention as something valuable rather than something to capture, manipulate, and monetize.

We reject:

* infinite feeds
* autoplay designed to prevent stopping
* unnecessary notifications
* engagement traps
* artificial urgency
* algorithmic compulsion
* interfaces designed primarily around maximizing session length

We favor:

* deliberate interaction
* clear interfaces
* explicit choices
* finite information
* search over endless feeds
* completion over retention

The goal is not to eliminate technology.

The goal is to make it possible to **put the technology down**.

---

## 3. The internet should be a resource, not a place to live

The internet contains an extraordinary amount of useful information.

But access to information and exposure to everything are not the same thing.

Modern internet services increasingly place recommendation systems, advertising, social metrics, outrage, trends, and entertainment between the person and the information they actually wanted.

We believe people should be able to construct a smaller, quieter version of the internet for themselves.

An internet where:

> **You decide what enters your world.**

Search should be possible without being surrounded by noise.

Information should be discoverable without requiring permanent participation.

Communication should be possible without requiring constant visibility.

The world's information can remain enormous while an individual's personal information environment remains small.

---

## 4. We can preserve the good parts of the past

There is value in the computing culture that existed before the modern attention economy.

Personal computers felt personal.

Software was often installed because you deliberately chose it.

People maintained collections of programs, games, music, photographs, documents, and files.

Websites were destinations rather than infinite streams.

You searched for things instead of being continuously presented with them.

Computers were tools for making things.

We do not romanticize the limitations of older technology.

We take the **principles that were good** and rebuild them using modern technology.

The goal is not to recreate 1995.

The goal is:

> **1990s computing values with 2020s and 2030s capabilities.**

---

## 5. Modern technology belongs in the system

Intentional Computing is not anti-technology.

It is not anti-internet.

It is not anti-AI.

It is not an argument that everything modern is bad.

Quite the opposite.

We want to use:

* modern processors
* modern graphics
* modern programming languages
* modern networking
* modern encryption
* modern AI
* modern development tools
* modern storage
* modern hardware

But we want these technologies to work **for the individual**.

The question is not:

> "Is this technology modern?"

The question is:

> **"Does this technology increase human capability without unnecessarily reducing human agency?"**

---

## 6. AI should reduce complexity, not manufacture it

AI is potentially one of the most powerful tools ever made available to an individual.

It can search enormous bodies of information.

It can explain difficult subjects.

It can write and understand software.

It can transform ideas into working prototypes.

It can summarize complexity.

It can act as an interface between humans and increasingly complicated systems.

We believe this is an enormous opportunity.

But AI should not simply become another mechanism for maximizing engagement.

The ideal AI assistant should help a person:

**decide → understand → create → accomplish → leave.**

Not:

**click → consume → react → scroll → repeat.**

AI should make the world more manageable.

It should not make the attention economy more efficient.

---

## 7. Local ownership matters

Whenever practical, software should allow people to own their data and understand where it lives.

We favor:

* local-first software
* open formats
* exportable data
* self-hosting where appropriate
* offline functionality where practical
* transparent configuration
* minimal dependencies
* repairability
* portability

Cloud services can be useful.

Online services can be useful.

But dependence should be a choice rather than an assumption.

A person's computer should still be capable of being **their computer**.

---

## 8. Software should be understandable

We believe software should not require blind trust.

Where practical, systems should be:

* inspectable
* configurable
* documented
* reproducible
* understandable
* replaceable

Complexity is sometimes unavoidable.

Unnecessary opacity is not.

The person using a tool should have a reasonable understanding of what the tool is doing on their behalf.

---

## 9. Personal environments should be configurable

There is no single correct way for everyone to use technology.

Some people enjoy social media.

Some people want constant connectivity.

Some people want highly automated systems.

Some people want a minimal computer that does only a few things.

Intentional Computing does not require everyone to make the same choices.

It provides the ability to **make your own choices**.

The fundamental principle is:

> **Your computing environment should reflect your values rather than somebody else's business model.**

---

## 10. Technology should help people make things

We favor computers as instruments of creation.

Write.

Program.

Draw.

Compose.

Build.

Research.

Design.

Repair.

Play.

Learn.

Experiment.

Create something that did not exist before.

Technology is at its best when it gives an individual capabilities that previously required an institution.

---

## 11. Preservation is progress

Old software, games, computers, formats, and creative works are part of computing history.

Preserving them is not merely nostalgia.

Understanding previous systems helps us understand what we gained, what we lost, and what we might want to build differently.

Emulation, digital preservation, open tooling, archival formats, and local collections are therefore part of the same philosophy.

We should be able to move forward without throwing away everything behind us.

---

## 12. The computer should disappear when the task is finished

This may be the simplest test.

If someone wants to research something, help them research it.

If they want to write software, help them write it.

If they want to create art, help them create it.

If they want to play a game, let them play it.

If they want to communicate with someone, connect them.

Then let them leave.

There is no requirement that the user remain inside the system.

**The objective is completion, not captivity.**

---

# The Future We Want

We believe the future does not have to choose between:

**going backward**

and

**accepting every new technology exactly as it arrives.**

There is another option.

We can take the best ideas from personal computing, the early internet, modern open-source software, and the latest advances in AI and combine them into something better.

A future where computers are more capable but less intrusive.

Where the internet is larger but our personal information environments are smaller.

Where AI is more powerful but the human remains in control.

Where software becomes more intelligent without becoming more manipulative.

Where people can choose how connected they want to be.

Where technology gives people more time rather than consuming the time they have.

Where the computer is once again primarily a **tool**.

---

# The Principle

We are not trying to stop the future.

We are trying to make sure the future remains worth living in.

> **Build powerful technology.**
>
> **Keep the human in control.**
>
> **Protect attention.**
>
> **Preserve ownership.**
>
> **Prefer intention over compulsion.**
>
> **Use AI to reduce complexity.**
>
> **Make technology serve the person.**
>
> **And always leave the door open for the user to walk away.**
